Compare the two cohorts below. For each category, state what the successful runs
did and what the failing runs did, as short behaviour descriptions.

{{taxonomy}}

Every behaviour description must carry at least one piece of evidence. Evidence
is a component that appears verbatim in one of the trials above: its trial id,
its `trajectory_component` label, its step ids, and its summary text copied
exactly. Each component is shown with its step range as `[first-last]`; write
`step_ids` as exactly those two numbers, so `[34-35]` becomes `[34, 35]`. Do
not cite a component that is not listed above, and do not invent step ids. A
behaviour you cannot cite is one you must omit.

Only include a category when the cohorts actually differ on it. Omit categories
where you see no difference; an empty comparison is a better answer than a
manufactured one.

Set `label` only on `behavior_discovery`, where it names the pattern you found.
Every other category must have `label` set to null. A label is two to four
plain English words naming ONE pattern -- `subagent delegation`, not
`subagent_delegation` and not `delegation_and_scripted_bulk_conversion`. No
underscores, no `and`: if you are joining two patterns with `and`, pick the one
the evidence supports better and drop the other.

Each `behavior_description` is ONE sentence, at most about twenty words, and
starts with the agent, not the cohort. The column the description sits in
already says which cohort it belongs to, so "Successful runs read the codebase
first" wastes its opening on something the reader can see. Write "Reads the
codebase once, then implements without re-planning." Say what was done, not
whether it was good.

Write all prose in short, plain sentences: one fact per sentence, active voice.
